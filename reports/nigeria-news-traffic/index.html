Reading it

Diaspora reach matters. Roughly a quarter of combined traffic comes from outside Nigeria, the US, UK, Canada and Germany alone add up to over 15%, pointing to a real diaspora readership worth factoring into any content or ad strategy aimed at these titles.

Reach alone is a shallow read. Punch and Vanguard have the most diaspora traffic in raw terms, but raw traffic doesn't say anything about whether that audience actually sticks around, reads deeply, or is receptive to a message once they land. The section below folds engagement quality in to get a properly weighted answer.

Reach vs. quality: a weighted diaspora value score

Composite of diaspora traffic volume + engagement depth

Traffic volume tells you how many diaspora visitors land on a site. It says nothing about whether they stay, read, or are in a receptive state when they get there, which is what actually matters for a real-estate lead-gen message. What this actually means for BuyLetLive: Punch is the safe #1 pick under any weighting since it's never below 2nd place, meaning it works whether the goal is broad diaspora awareness or targeted lead-gen. The real finding is SaharaReporters: at balanced weighting it's already statistically tied with Vanguard (53.3 vs 50.4) despite having roughly a fifth of Vanguard's diaspora traffic, and under a lead-gen-weighted view, which matches BuyLetLive's stated "maximize enquiries" objective, it actually overtakes Vanguard entirely (72.0 vs 37.1). Its readers stay nearly 4× longer, view more pages, and bounce at roughly two-thirds the rate. For a real-estate message that needs someone to read, click through, and fill a form, not just glance and scroll past, that engaged-but-smaller audience may convert better per impression than a bigger, shallower one. Vanguard's high reach is real, but it comes with the weakest engagement of the top-3 sites, making it a "broad but shallow" audience. BusinessDay and ThisDay are hard to justify on either reach or quality for diaspora targeting specifically, despite ThisDay's higher share of its own traffic being diaspora (a concentration metric, not a value metric, since its absolute diaspora pool is simply too small at ~202K visits/month).
Limitation to flag: the engagement metrics above (visit duration, pages/visit, bounce rate) are each site's global averages, not diaspora-specific. Similarweb paywalls country-level engagement breakdowns behind a plan upgrade (confirmed: both the "Traffic & engagement" and "Audience" pages redirect back to the free overview under the current expired trial). It's a reasonable proxy since there's no strong reason to expect UK/US readers behave dramatically differently from a site's average reader, but it's an assumption, not a directly-measured diaspora number. If this decision is worth real ad budget, a Similarweb Pro upgrade (or a UTM-tagged test campaign on 2 to 3 of these sites) would confirm it directly.

Marketing channels

Jul 2026 · How visits arrive

Direct and organic search dominate across the board, paid channels are negligible for all five. ThisDay leads the group on organic search share (40.96%), while BusinessDay draws the (still tiny, <1%) most paid-search traffic of the set.

01

Organic search: thisdaylive.com leads at 40.96% of its traffic, the strongest SEO-driven acquisition of the five.

02

Paid search: businessday.ng leads, but at under 1% of total traffic, none of these sites meaningfully invest in paid acquisition.

03

Referrals: the group's top inbound referrers are c.newsnow.com (23.4% of referral traffic), dailypost.ng (11.3%), punchng.com itself (11.2%), and bbc.com (6.5%), aggregators and cross-media links carry real weight.

04

Display ads: vanguardngr.com leads the group on display-ad-driven traffic, at 1.35% share, still a minor channel overall.

Takeaways

→

Punch is the clear #1 for diaspora targeting under any weighting of reach vs. engagement, high volume, above-average engagement, and disproportionately strong in the UK and Canada specifically (54.8% and 57.2% of those markets' traffic to this group).

→

SaharaReporters is the standout for a lead-gen-weighted view, its diaspora audience is small (~288K/mo across UK/US/CA/DE) but reads nearly 4× longer, views more pages, and bounces two-thirds as often as Vanguard's. For BuyLetLive's "maximize enquiries" objective specifically, it likely outperforms Vanguard per impression despite the smaller pool.

→

Vanguard is broad but shallow, the 2nd-largest diaspora traffic pool, but the weakest engagement of the top three; a good fit for pure reach/awareness buys, a weaker fit for conversion-focused placements.

→

BusinessDay and ThisDay are hard to justify for diaspora spend on current data, smallest diaspora pools and, for BusinessDay, the weakest engagement in the group. ThisDay's high organic-search share (40.96%) and desktop lean (21.7%) suggest a more professional readership, but its diaspora volume is too small to prioritize yet.

→

Direct + organic search drive almost all traffic industry-wide; paid acquisition is essentially unused across all five sites, an open channel if any of them wanted to invest in it, and a data point worth knowing before proposing a paid partnership.
